Privacy

What this demo does with data, what a real version would read from your Spotify export, and where that data should and should not go.

This Demo

Everything you see is generated. It is not connected to a Spotify account, it reads no files, and it sends nothing about you anywhere. It does not use cookies or browser storage.

What a Real Version Would Read

The demo copies the shape of Spotify's extended streaming history. A real version would rely on these fields:

  • ts The time a play ended.
  • ms_played How long you listened.
  • skipped Whether you skipped it.
  • platform The kind of device, such as phone or laptop.
  • conn_country The country the connection came from.
  • ip_addr The IP address of the connection.
  • master_metadata_track_name The song title.
  • master_metadata_album_artist_name The artist.

Spotify has renamed fields before. Open one file and check the names before wiring anything up.

Where Your Files Should Stay

Your export contains years of listening, with timestamps and an IP address. When this becomes a real app, the files should be read in your browser and never uploaded to a server.

Your IP Address and Places

The export includes the IP address for each play. The Listening Map turns that into an approximate city. A real version should do that lookup on your device, from data shipped with the app, because sending addresses to an outside lookup service would share where you listened.

Search History

If you request the account data package too, past exports have included a search queries file. It is optional, and a real version should treat it as sensitive.

Moods Are Guesses

Moods, forecasts, and playlists are inferred from genre, time of day, and how you played songs. They are not measurements of how you feel, and they are not a diagnosis. Treat them as a prompt for reflection.

Links and Fonts

Artist links open a Spotify search in a new tab, so Spotify receives that search under its own policies. This page also loads fonts from Google Fonts, so Google receives the usual request details, like your IP address.

Getting Your Export

The steps are on the main page, under Use Your Real Listening History. Spotify's own privacy page controls the export.

Use Your Real Listening History

This page runs on generated data. To see your own days, you need Spotify's data export. It has to be requested on a computer.

  1. Open Spotify's Account Privacy page. Sign in if it asks.
    Open Spotify privacy settings
  2. Scroll to "Download your data". You will see a few checkboxes for different packages.
  3. Tick "Extended streaming history". This is the package with your full play history, with timestamps, skips, and connection details.
  4. Tick "Account data" as well for search history. It is a separate package, and past exports have included a search queries file in it.
  5. Select Request, then confirm in the email Spotify sends. Spotify does not start until you click the confirm link.
  6. Wait for the second email. Spotify says it can take up to 30 days. It often arrives in a few days.
  7. Download the ZIP and unzip it. The play history is a set of JSON files.
