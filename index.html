Your Micro Genres

Each dot is a micro genre, placed by this app's own energy and positivity estimates for that genre. Circles are genres that lean alt, and diamonds lean popular, based on invented popularity scores. Bigger shapes were played more. Dashed outlines were not played in this range.

Your Music Horoscope

Which 2020s music archetype your listening matches. It reads the same date range as your genres, and it is only for fun.

The archetypes are made up by this prototype and scored with its own rules, using simulated plays. They nod to trends described in Wikipedia's 2020s in music article (CC BY-SA 4.0). This is not a personality test.

What You Skip

The share of plays you skipped, one month at a time. Small groups are left out so one skipped song does not top the list. Select a side card, or use the arrows, to bring it to the middle.

Where you listen

Cities and countries ranked by your listening time. In this prototype the places are invented. In a real version the country would come from the export and the city from an approximate IP lookup. This shows where you were when you played music, so it describes your own listening, not other listeners.

This Prototype

Everything you see is generated. It is not connected to Spotify, it cannot read your files yet, and it sends nothing about you anywhere. It does not use cookies or browser storage. The artists, songs, albums, places, and plays are all made up.

What Spotify's Export Contains

A real version would read Spotify's extended streaming history, which you request from your account's privacy page. Each play is one record. This prototype is modeled on these fields:

  • ts The time a play ended, in UTC.
  • ms_played How long you listened.
  • skipped Whether you skipped it. Some records leave this empty.
  • platform A text value such as ios or an operating system name. A real version would have to sort these into phone, laptop, and so on, and it cannot tell every kind of device, such as a speaker.
  • conn_country The country the connection came from.
  • ip_addr The IP address of the connection. Some exports call this ip_addr_decrypted.
  • master_metadata_track_name The song title.
  • master_metadata_album_artist_name The artist.
  • master_metadata_album_album_name The album.
  • spotify_track_uri An ID that identifies the track.

Some records have no track name, for example some podcast or video plays. Spotify has changed the format before, so open one file and check the field names before building on it.

What the Export Does Not Contain

Several things on these pages are not in the export. Here is what this prototype does about each one.

  • Genres. Not in the export. This prototype uses a hand-written genre list. A real version needs another source. Spotify's API has had a genres field on artists, but it has been narrowing access, so check what is available before relying on it.
  • Popularity. Spotify removed artist and track popularity from its Web API for Development Mode apps in February 2026. The popular-versus-alt split here uses invented scores, so a real version needs a different measure.
  • Energy and positivity. Spotify's audio features stopped working for new apps in November 2024. The numbers here come from a hand-written table by genre, so a real version has no Spotify source for them.
  • Heart rate and activity. Not in Spotify's export. The Apple Watch numbers in the genre breakdown are generated. A real version would need your Apple Health data, described below.
  • Artist photos and cover art. Not in the export. This prototype draws its own.
  • City. The export has a country code and an IP address. Turning an address into a city needs an IP location database, which Spotify does not provide.

Apple Watch Data (Simulated)

A web page cannot read Apple Health directly. A real version would need you to export your Health data from the Health app (your profile picture, then Export All Health Data). That produces a ZIP with a file called export.xml, which holds heart rate and active energy records and workouts. A real version could match those to your plays by timestamp, on your device. Health data is sensitive, so it should never be uploaded. Matching by time shows what was happening while you listened. It does not show that the music caused it, and none of it is medical advice.

Moods Are Guesses

The music horoscope works the same way. Its ten archetypes are invented for this prototype and scored from the simulated plays. They are loosely inspired by trends described in Wikipedia's article on 2020s music, which is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0, and they are for fun, not a personality test.

The mood colors, forecasts, and playlists come from rules written for this prototype: genre estimates, time of day, weekday, device, and skipping. They are not Spotify data, they are not measurements of how you feel, and they are not a diagnosis. Treat them as a prompt for reflection.

Why Files Should Stay on Your Device

Your export contains years of listening, with timestamps and an IP address. A real version should read the files in your browser and never upload them to a server. This also avoids Spotify's API limits. Since February 2026, a new app in Development Mode is limited to five authorized users and needs a Premium account for its owner. Spotify's current rules are on its developer site and may change.

Your IP Address and Places

A real version would turn each play's IP address into an approximate city. It should do that on your device, using data shipped with the app, because sending addresses to an outside lookup service would share where you listened.

Links and Fonts

Artist links open a Spotify search in a new tab, so Spotify receives that search under its own policies. This page also loads fonts from Google Fonts, so Google receives the usual request details, like your IP address.

Getting Your Export

The steps are on the main page, under Getting Your Spotify Data. Spotify's own privacy page controls the export.

Getting Your Spotify Data

This prototype cannot read your files yet. These are the steps to request the export a real version would use. Spotify may change them.

  1. Open Spotify's Account Privacy page. Sign in if it asks.
    Open Spotify privacy settings
  2. Find the section for downloading your data. You will see a few checkboxes for different packages.
  3. Tick "Extended streaming history". This is the package with your full play history, with timestamps, play length, skips, device type, country, and IP address.
  4. Select Request, then confirm in the email Spotify sends. Spotify does not start until you click the confirm link.
  5. Wait for the email with your download link. Spotify says it can take up to 30 days.
  6. Download the ZIP and unzip it. The play history is a set of JSON files, usually named Streaming_History_Audio followed by numbers.
